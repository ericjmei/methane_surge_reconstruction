\begin{center}\large Chemistry and pandemic activity in Laughner et al. (2021)\end{center}
\noindent\textbf{Status: main text read; supplementary methods unread and requested.}
This provisional note does not satisfy the requested full-paper review.
\textbf{Reference.} Laughner et al., \emph{PNAS} 118, e2109481118,
\href{https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2109481118}{doi:10.1073/pnas.2109481118}.

\paragraph{Main-text method.} The paper synthesizes observations and model
experiments on pandemic changes in human activity, emissions, air quality,
and greenhouse gases. Its methane example compares deseasonalized TCCON column
observations at Park Falls and Lauder with a two-hemisphere box-model scenario.
The observational reference is a robust linear trend over 2016--2019;
the box model holds methane emissions constant after 2012 and perturbs OH in
2020. Thus it is neither a 2019-baseline flux inversion nor a posterior global
methane budget. The broader study also uses MIROC--CHASER assimilation to
diagnose ozone production efficiency and GEOS--Chem experiments for nitrate
particulate matter.

\paragraph{Main-text findings.} The authors report that 2020 methane columns
at the two TCCON sites rise about 0.3\% above their extrapolated earlier trends.
A 3\% OH decrease, bracketed by scenarios of 2--4\%, yields a similar methane
enhancement in the box model. They interpret this agreement as evidence that
reduced NO$_x$ emissions and the associated OH decrease could contribute to
methane growth. They explicitly allow changes in methane sources as well.
The prediction that growth should return toward its earlier rate after NO$_x$
recovers is conditional on OH being the dominant 2020 perturbation; it is not
a constraint inferred from 2021--2022 methane observations.

\paragraph{Interpretation for the surge comparison.} A scenario selected to
explain an enhancement above a 2016--2019 trend cannot be inserted directly
as a 2019-based flux anomaly. Doing so would require the box-model initial
state, baseline OH and other loss terms, and the time profile of the imposed
perturbation. Those details belong to the unavailable supplement. The main-text
3\% OH scenario is retained as context, with no numerical conversion to an
equivalent emission forcing and no invented posterior uncertainty.
