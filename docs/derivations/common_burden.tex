\section*{A common-burden sensitivity calculation}
Native reconstruction budgets require native burden and loss-frequency series.
When only an annual OH removal flux $Q_j$ is supplied, introduce an explicitly
conditional reference burden $m^*_j=cX_j$, in which $X_j$ is NOAA annual mean
surface methane and $c=2.77$ Tg ppb$^{-1}$ is He's conversion factor. Define
\begin{equation}
F^*_{\rm OH,j}=-m^*_0\left(\frac{Q_j}{m^*_j}-\frac{Q_0}{m^*_0}\right)
=Q_0-Q_j\frac{X_0}{X_j}.
\end{equation}
This is He's baseline-burden forcing evaluated with a common observational
proxy. It is not a native model diagnostic: surface and whole-atmosphere
abundances and their temporal evolution can differ. A constant multiplicative
error in $c$ cancels; a time-varying representativeness error does not.
For observed growth $G^*_j=c g_j$, the source-plus-OH forcing required under
fixed soil and other chemical loss frequencies is
\begin{equation}
F^*_{\rm req,j}=c(g_j-g_0)+\frac{c(X_j-X_0)}{\tau_0}.
\end{equation}
We examine chemical lifetimes $\tau_0=9,10,11,12$ years as a sensitivity range,
not as posterior estimates. It brackets the approximately 11-year OH lifetimes
in He and Qu and allows for additional chemical removal. Soil uptake is excluded
from $\tau_0$, following He. Annual mean burden is an approximation to the
burden in an annual integrated budget. We therefore do not fit these scenarios
to the observed growth or treat discrepancies as a new flux inversion.
