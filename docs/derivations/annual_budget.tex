Following equation (1) of He et al. (2026,
\href{https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adz9007}{doi:10.1126/sciadv.adz9007}),
the annual global methane budget is
\begin{equation}
 G_j=E_j-(k_{\mathrm{OH},j}+k_{\mathrm{other},j})m_j-L_j,
\end{equation}
in which $G_j$ is atmospheric mass growth, $E_j$ is gross emission,
$L_j$ is positive soil uptake (all in Tg CH$_4$ yr$^{-1}$), $m_j$ is
atmospheric methane mass (Tg CH$_4$), and the $k$ terms are chemical loss
frequencies (yr$^{-1}$). The subscript 0 denotes 2019 and every $\Delta$
denotes the value in year $j$ minus its 2019 value. Soil uptake must not
also be included in $k_{\mathrm{other}}$.

Writing $k_0=k_{\mathrm{OH},0}+k_{\mathrm{other},0}$ and expanding gives
\begin{equation}
 G_j=\underbrace{G_0-k_0\Delta m_j}_{R_j}
     +\underbrace{\Delta E_j}_{F_{E,j}}
     \underbrace{-m_0\Delta k_{\mathrm{OH},j}}_{F_{\mathrm{OH},j}}
     +\epsilon_j,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
 \epsilon_j=-m_0\Delta k_{\mathrm{other},j}
 -(\Delta k_{\mathrm{OH},j}+\Delta k_{\mathrm{other},j})\Delta m_j
 -\Delta L_j.
\end{equation}
He et al. neglect $\epsilon_j$. The implementation retains it as a
diagnostic, separately from the three published attribution terms.
$R_j$ is the residual growth under baseline rates evaluated at the
actual burden. It is a growth contribution, and is therefore generally
nonzero in 2019. Its anomaly is $-k_0\Delta m_j$. The forced growth
anomaly is
\begin{equation}
 F_j=\Delta G_j+k_0\Delta m_j
 \simeq F_{E,j}+F_{\mathrm{OH},j}.
\end{equation}
The positive adjustment $k_0\Delta m_j$ removes the extra loss caused
by methane accumulation. A percentage change in an arbitrarily weighted
OH mean is not sufficient to calculate $\Delta k_{\mathrm{OH}}$.
The chemical lifetime must be methane-reaction weighted.

For a published OH removal flux $Q_j=k_{\mathrm{OH},j}m_j$, the exact
conversion to the baseline-burden OH forcing is
\begin{equation}
 F_{\mathrm{OH},j}
 =\frac{-\Delta Q_j+k_{\mathrm{OH},0}\Delta m_j}
 {1+\Delta m_j/m_0}.
\end{equation}
Thus, a change in removal flux must not be substituted directly for
the change in loss frequency. This conversion requires the burden and
baseline removal rate, and will not be made where those are unavailable.

These equations apply to globally integrated budgets. Regional methane
growth also includes transport, so no regional relaxation adjustment
will be inferred from local emissions alone. Published regional emission
anomalies can be compared separately. No inversion is performed.
